## Same law, zoomed in

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{J}$$

The curl of $\mathbf{B}$ at a point is set by the current density *at that point*. Stokes' theorem converts this to and from $\oint\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{l}=\mu_0 I_{\text{enc}}$.

## Reading it

Outside a wire, $\mathbf{B}$ circles the wire — yet $\nabla\times\mathbf{B}=0$ there, because no current flows there. Inside the wire the curl is $\mu_0\mathbf{J}$. Curl is local; it is about what happens *at the point*, not about whether the lines look like they go around.

## Its twin and its future

This is the magnetic counterpart of $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}=\rho_v/\varepsilon_0$: the cell with a source in it. Both say "the field is produced here, by this."

It is also one of the two cells that change in time-varying fields. A changing electric field acts like a current — Maxwell's displacement current — and the law becomes $\nabla\times\mathbf{B}=\mu_0\mathbf{J}+\mu_0\varepsilon_0\,\partial\mathbf{E}/\partial t$. That added term, together with Faraday's law in the $\nabla\times\mathbf{E}$ cell, is what lets electromagnetic waves exist.
