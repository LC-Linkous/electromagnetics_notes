## The one equation with both fields

$$\mathbf{F} = q\left(\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B}\right)$$

Everything else in this course is about one field or the other. The Lorentz force is where they meet: a charge feels $\mathbf{E}$ whether or not it moves, and feels $\mathbf{B}$ only through its velocity.

In the Maxwell grid this is the bridge between the E row and the B row. In statics it is the *only* bridge — the four field equations never mention the other field. (In time-varying fields they do, through Faraday's law and displacement current.)

## The two halves behave differently

- The electric part does work: it can speed a charge up or slow it down.
- The magnetic part never does work. $\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B}$ is perpendicular to $\mathbf{u}$, so it can only turn the velocity, never change its magnitude. A charge in a uniform $\mathbf{B}$ with no $\mathbf{E}$ moves in a circle (or a helix) at constant speed.

## Direction of the magnetic force

Right hand: fingers along $\mathbf{u}$, curl them toward $\mathbf{B}$, thumb gives $\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B}$. That is the force on a *positive* charge; reverse it for a negative one. Most sign errors in this unit are here.

## From charges to wires

Sum the magnetic force over all the moving charges in a wire segment and you get $d\mathbf{F}=I\,d\mathbf{l}\times\mathbf{B}$ — the force on a current element, and from there the force between two wires and the torque on a current loop. The magnetic dipole moment, and so all of magnetic materials, starts from that torque.
