## No magnetic charge

$$\oint_S \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = 0$$

The net magnetic flux out of *any* closed surface is zero. Magnetic field lines never start and never end; whatever enters a closed surface leaves it. There is nothing for them to start on — no magnetic charge has ever been found.

This is the magnetic counterpart of $\oint\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l}=0$: the two "equals zero" cells in the Maxwell grid.

## Open surfaces still matter

Through a closed surface the flux is always zero. Through an **open** surface — the cross-section of a coil — it is not, and that flux

$$\Psi = \int_S \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$$

is the quantity inductance is built on: flux linkage $\lambda = N\Psi$ per ampere.

## Its differential form

Shrink the surface and the divergence theorem gives $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0$, which in turn guarantees that $\mathbf{B}$ can be written as the curl of a vector potential $\mathbf{A}$. This is the one Maxwell equation that stays exactly as it is when fields vary in time.
